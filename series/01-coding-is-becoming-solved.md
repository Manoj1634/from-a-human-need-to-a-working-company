# Coding Is Becoming a Solved Problem. What Comes Next?

*Part 1 of From a Human Need to a Working Company*

---

I'm an AI engineer. For years, the hardest part of my job was writing the code that solved the problem.

That's changing fast. AI writes a lot of my code now. I describe what I need, review what comes back, and ship in a fraction of the time it used to take. The *how* of building is getting cheaper every month.

That doesn't make engineers less useful. It moves the hard part somewhere else.

When code is no longer the bottleneck, different questions start to matter:

- **Which problem is worth solving?**
- **Who has that problem, and would they pay to make it go away?**
- **How do you make the value obvious to someone who doesn't care how it works?**
- **What does it take to go from one person solving it to a team solving it for many people?**

Those are business questions, and most engineers were never taught them. We learned to solve problems. We didn't learn to find them, price them, sell them, or build a company around them.

## Who this is for

- **Engineers and developers** who are watching AI write more of their code and wondering where their value moves next.
- **People who want to build their own company** and are wondering what it takes beyond a good idea or a good product.
- **Consultants and forward-deployed engineers** who help businesses and want to understand where those businesses, and their markets, are going.

You don't need a business background. If you can debug a system, you can learn how a company works.

## Why it might help

Engineers already think in systems. We break problems into small pieces, define what "done" looks like, ship in versions, and fix the one broken part when something fails.

A business runs on the same thinking, pointed at customers, value, selling and teams rather than only at code.

If you can make that shift, you stop being only the person who builds what someone else decided. You also become someone who helps decide what is worth building. As AI makes code cheaper, that skill becomes rarer and more valuable.
