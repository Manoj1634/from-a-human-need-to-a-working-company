# One Person, Every Seat: Why a Company Works Better Together

*Part 5 of From a Human Need to a Working Company*

---

Imagine a man selling balloons to cars stopped in traffic. A kid in a back seat points at the window, the parent rolls it down, and a few dollars change hands.

Look at him the way you'd look at a company. He holds every job himself:

- **CEO:** he chose the product and the spot.
- **Engineering:** none. He buys from a supplier and resells.
- **Sales:** the wave at the window, timed to the traffic.
- **Finance:** he knows how many he has to sell to make the day worth it.
- **Operations:** which junction, which hours, when it's busiest.
- **Customer success:** none. Nobody comes back, so nobody is kept.
- **Legal and security:** none. Nothing protects him on a bad day.

He is a company of one. That's exactly why he can survive but can't grow. Every hour he spends on one job is taken from the others, and the empty seats are where things break.

I was in a smaller version of his position once, when I sold digital marketing services as a student. I could do the work. But I was also the salesperson, the marketer, the finance team and the operations team, and each of those seats got only a fraction of my time.

## The seats of a company

A company fills every seat with someone who owns one question.

| Seat | The question it owns |
|---|---|
| **CEO** | Where are we going, and what do we bet on? |
| **Strategy / Product** | Where is the market moving, and why us? |
| **Engineering** | Can we build it, and will it keep working? |
| **Marketing** | Do the right people know about us and understand us? |
| **Sales** | Will they exchange money for it? |
| **Customer Success** | Did the value land, and will they stay? |
| **Finance** | Can we afford this, and will we survive? |
| **Operations / People** | Do we have the people, tools and process? |
| **Legal / Security** | Are we protected from outside risk? |

## A company has needs, like a person

A company has the same basic needs as a person:

- **Food:** cash coming in, from sales and finance.
- **Health:** systems that work, from engineering and operations.
- **Shelter:** protection, from legal and security.
- **Family:** trust and people who want to stay, from culture and people.
- **Senses:** seeing the market, from strategy and product.
- **Voice:** being heard, from marketing.
- **Brain:** deciding, from the CEO.

A company fails the same way a person does: when one of its needs goes unmet for too long. A great product with no cash starves. Great sales with a broken product gets sick.

## The seats work as one loop

The seats aren't separate machines. They form one loop around the customer:

```
Strategy sees the problem → CEO bets → Finance funds → Operations staffs
     ↑                                                        ↓
Success delivers  ←  Sales closes  ←  Marketing tells  ←  Engineering builds
```

What customers say flows back to strategy, and the loop starts again, a little smarter each time.

## Two ideas worth knowing

**A manager's output is the team's output.** Andy Grove, who ran Intel, defined a manager's output as the output of the organization they run plus the groups they influence. Leaders are measured by leverage, not by how hard they work personally.

**The org chart becomes the product.** Melvin Conway observed that systems end up mirroring the communication structure of the teams that build them. If the seats don't talk, the product has seams. As an AI engineer, I see the same thing in multi-agent systems: how you split up the agents shapes what they produce.

## Try this

List the seats in your own work, or in a project you're part of. Next to each one, write who fills it: you, a teammate, an AI agent, or nobody.

The seats marked "nobody" are where your next problem will come from.
